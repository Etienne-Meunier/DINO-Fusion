\section*{Summary}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Goal: generate the post-spin-up $(T, S)$ state of the Veros ACC channel directly from the EKE-closure
        coefficients $(c_k, c_\epsilon)$, skipping spin-up.
  \item Pipeline: DINO-Fusion diffusion code adapted to a parameter ensemble; runs end to end on Jean Zay in
        under one A100 hour.
  \item Result: with per-level normalisation the model's hold-out error is $\approx$ the mean-state
        baseline (\SI{0.15}{K}); it beats the nearest training run only where the hold-out gap is large, and ties
        copying the row below when extrapolating one row.
        A Wasserstein metric on the stratification profiles gives the same ranking and shows that training
        and held-out conditions score alike.
  \item Per-level min-max normalisation (data range $\to [-1,1]$), tested on the same three splits, is worse
        (0.20 / 0.23 / \SI{0.29}{K}): the ensemble spread halves and the bias dominates (Table~\ref{tab:splits}).
  \item Physical miss: the model generates temperature inversions at the rate of the weakly eddying runs
        in every regime.
\end{itemize}

\section{Data}
\begin{itemize}
  \item 100 Veros ACC runs, $10\times10$ log-spaced grid: $c_k\in[0.0125,\,0.8]$,
        $c_\epsilon\in[0.0875,\,5.6]$; 100 years, 30-day output, interior grid $30\times42\times15$ (x, y, z).
  \item Kept: $T$ and $S$, last 20 years (241 snapshots/run). $S = \SI{35}{psu}$ everywhere in every run.
  \item Nearly deterministic end states: within-window std \SI{0.01}{K} above \SI{1400}{m} vs
        \SI{0.05}{K} between grid neighbours; bottom level still drifts by \SI{0.45}{K} over the window.
  \item One effective parameter: $\;\log c_k - 1.05\,\log c_\epsilon\;$ explains 99\,\% of the variance of
        the domain-mean $T$; the low-$c_k/c_\epsilon$ part of the grid saturates.
  \item Per-level distribution of $T$ (Figure~\ref{fig:tdist}): above \SI{400}{m} a mixture of restoring values
        and interior, with $\sigma_z$ set by the meridional gradient, so $\mu_z \pm 3\sigma_z$ is wider than the
        level and the normalised values stay in $[-0.7,\,0.4]$; below \SI{1400}{m} a warm tail to $x' = +2.7$.
        Run-to-run signal $\sigma_z^{\mathrm{runs}}$: 0.7\,\% of $3\sigma_z$ at the surface, 26\,\% at the bottom.
\end{itemize}

\section{Method}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Fields $\to$ 30 channels $(T_{1..15}, S_{1..15})$, normalised per level, land $\to 0$,
        zero-padded $42\times30 \to 48\times32$:
        \begin{equation}
          x'_{z} = \frac{x_{z} - \mu_{z}}{3\,\max(\sigma_{z}, 0.05)} .
          \label{eq:norm}
        \end{equation}
        $\mu_z, \sigma_z$ are computed once on all 100 runs and held fixed for every hold-out split (a deliberate,
        mild leakage of 30 scaling constants; no held-out state enters training). The sampler clips the predicted clean state at $|x'| \le 1$ (three standard deviations) at every step.
        Alternative tested: per-level min-max, $x'_z = 2\,(x_z - \min_z)/(\max_z - \min_z) - 1$, with land and
        padding cells at the normalised level mean instead of 0 (Table~\ref{tab:splits}, last rows).
  \item Denoiser: UNet2DModel (64, 64, 128, 128), 6.9\,M parameters. The condition $c$, the standardised
        pair $(\log c_k, \log c_\epsilon)$, enters through an MLP added to the timestep embedding.
  \item DDPM, $\epsilon$-prediction, 1000 steps, squared-cosine schedule, EMA:
        \begin{equation}
          \mathcal L(\theta) = \mathbb E_{t,\,x_0,\,\epsilon}\,
          \big\| \epsilon - \epsilon_\theta\big(\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\,x_0 + \sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\,\epsilon,\; t,\; c\big) \big\|^2 .
        \end{equation}
        20\,000 steps, batch 32, AdamW $10^{-4}$ with cosine decay; \SI{22}{min} on one A100.
  \item Sampling: 1000-step DDPM loop with EMA weights; land and padding re-zeroed after every step;
        32 samples per condition.
\end{itemize}

\section{Evaluation}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Truth: 20-year time-mean state of each held-out run; metrics on water cells.
  \item Baselines: mean training state; nearest training run in standardised log-parameter space;
        average of the axis-neighbour training runs on the grid.
  \item Three hold-out splits, same data file and statistics, different training sets:
        scattered (10 interior points), band of three rows ($c_k$ = 0.126, 0.2, 0.3175; 30 runs; training rows
        a factor 6.3 apart), top row ($c_k$ = 0.8; 10 runs; extrapolation).
  \item Distributional metric. Each state is reduced to its profile of horizontal means over water cells,
        $\bar T_z$. Per level, the Wasserstein-1 distance between the 32 generated values and all 241 true
        snapshots of the window, through the quantile functions,
        \begin{equation}
          W_1^{(z)} = \int_0^1 \big| F_{g,z}^{-1}(q) - F_{t,z}^{-1}(q) \big|\, dq, \qquad
          W_1 = \sum_z \frac{\Delta z_z}{H}\, W_1^{(z)} ,
        \end{equation}
        with $\Delta z_z$ the level thicknesses from the level midpoints ($H = \SI{2080}{m}$).
        All 241 snapshots are used, not a subsample of 32: the quantile form accepts unequal sizes and a
        subsample would only add noise. A point prediction $x$ (the baselines) gives
        $W_1^{(z)} = \frac{1}{241}\sum_i |x_z - \bar T_{z,i}|$. Unlike the RMSE of the ensemble mean, this rewards a correct
        spread and does not reward collapse to the mean; it ignores horizontal structure, which the RMSE keeps.
\end{itemize}

\section{Findings}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Conditioning is weak under per-level scaling.} Signal-to-range ratio
        $\rho_z = \sigma_z^{\mathrm{runs}} / (3\sigma_z)$ is 0.7\,\% at the surface, 9\,\% at \SI{650}{m},
        25\,\% at the bottom; the $\epsilon$-loss weights all cells equally, so the shared structure dominates.
        Scattered split: RMSE = mean-state baseline (Figure~\ref{fig:scat}).
  \item \textbf{Model error is split-independent (0.14--\SI{0.17}{K}); baselines degrade with the gap
        (0.02--\SI{0.17}{K}).} The model beats the nearest run in the upper band row (0.127 vs \SI{0.152}{K}), ties
        the middle one, loses the lower one, and beats copying the row below over the trending part of the top row
        ($c_\epsilon$ 0.14--0.56: 0.12--0.23 vs 0.18--\SI{0.31}{K}) while losing at the corner and in the flat
        regime (Figures~\ref{fig:band}, \ref{fig:top}).
  \item \textbf{Depth structure.} Beats the nearest run between 370 and \SI{800}{m} in the band split
        (\SI{0.08}{K} vs \SI{0.14}{K} at \SI{650}{m}) and between 260 and \SI{1430}{m} in the top row; worse than
        baselines in the top \SI{250}{m} (scale $3\sigma_z \approx \SI{12}{K}$ amplifies sampler noise) and at the two
        bottom levels (skewed, drifting training distribution).
  \item \textbf{The sampler's clip is a regulariser, not a range limit.} Same trained models, sampling
        only: clip at $|x'|\le 1$ gives 0.152 / \SI{0.173}{K} (scattered / top row), at 1.5 it gives
        0.194 / \SI{0.183}{K}, at 3 it gives 0.302 / \SI{0.277}{K}, although the final samples barely
        exceed $|x'| = 1$ (0.1\,\% of values). Clipping the early, inaccurate clean-state estimates keeps
        the chain on track. The price is a range limit for extrapolation: with the fixed statistics 12\,\%
        of the held-out top row's true bottom values lie above $\mu_z + 3\sigma_z$ and cannot be generated.
  \item \textbf{Min-max normalisation is worse on every split} (0.199 / 0.233 / \SI{0.288}{K} against
        0.152 / 0.135 / 0.173; $W_1$ 0.165 / 0.188 / 0.241 against 0.127 / 0.111 / 0.143) and at almost every level:
        the 100--\SI{400}{m} band doubles (0.13 $\to$ \SI{0.24}{K} at \SI{106}{m}, scattered) and the bottom level goes
        0.34 $\to$ 0.43 / 0.28 $\to$ 0.54 / 0.35 $\to$ \SI{0.66}{K}. The ensemble spread halves (0.05 vs \SI{0.11}{K}) and a
        single sample scores like the ensemble mean: the error is bias, not sampler noise. Stretching the upper
        levels onto $[-1,1]$ gives the fixed structure of Figure~\ref{fig:tdist} more weight, not the run-to-run
        signal (1.4\,\% of the scale); stretching the bottom scale to the corner cell dilutes the level that
        carries the most signal.
  \item \textbf{Inversions.} Generated 10--12\,\% of interfaces with $\partial_z T < 0$ in every regime;
        truth: 7.7\,\% (scattered) $\to$ 1.3\,\% (band) $\to$ 0.1\,\% (top). The regime dependence is not
        learned.
  \item \textbf{$W_1$ on the mean profiles agrees with the RMSE ranking and adds two facts}
        (right-hand panels of Figures~\ref{fig:scat}--\ref{fig:top}).
        (i) Training grid points score the same as held-out ones (0.124 vs \SI{0.127}{K}, scattered split):
        the model does not fit the training conditions either, the signature of the mean-state collapse.
        (ii) Around \SI{650}{m} the samples beat the baselines under $W_1$ in the band and top splits
        (0.06 vs 0.12 and 0.08 vs \SI{0.17}{K}), because a point prediction is charged for the spread it cannot represent; the model's total
        is set by the top \SI{250}{m} and the bottom two levels, as for the RMSE.
  \item \textbf{Plumbing.} $S$ within \SI{0.02}{psu} of 35 with no constraint; land exact; ensemble spread
        $3.5\times$ the true within-window spread (Figures~\ref{fig:samples}--\ref{fig:samples_top}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Next steps}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Stratification constraint at sampling time (DINO-Fusion's isotonic projection, on $T$ since $S$ is
        constant): targets the inversion miss directly.
  \item Stronger conditioning: classifier-free guidance; year as a third condition (drift of \SI{0.45}{K}
        at the bottom inside the window).
  \item Evaluate against snapshots as well as the time mean (nearest-snapshot RMSE, spread--skill).
  \item Then: same pipeline on a global Veros configuration (active $S$, seasonal cycle, multi-century spin-up).
\end{itemize}
