\section*{Summary}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Goal: generate the post-spin-up $(T, S)$ state of the Veros ACC channel directly from the EKE-closure
        coefficients $(c_k, c_\epsilon)$, skipping spin-up.
  \item Pipeline: DINO-Fusion diffusion code adapted to a parameter ensemble; runs end to end on Jean Zay in
        under one A100 hour.
  \item Result: hold-out RMSE 0.061 / 0.076 / \SI{0.141}{K} (scattered / band of three rows / top row) against
        the nearest training run 0.037 / 0.116 / 0.170 and the mean state 0.155 / 0.197 / \SI{0.470}{K}: the model
        beats the nearest run in the two large-gap splits and loses only to the one-step neighbours of the
        scattered split. A Wasserstein metric on the stratification profiles gives the same ranking.
  \item The sampler's land/padding constraint was the main error source: re-imposing exact zeros after every
        step (DINO's constraint) instead of the noised fill the network saw in training cost 0.03--\SI{0.09}{K}
        (0.152 / 0.135 / \SI{0.173}{K} before; same models, sampling only).
  \item Per-level min-max normalisation was tested and gives the same result as 3-std under the same sampler;
        dropped.
  \item Physical miss: the model generates temperature inversions at the rate of the weakly eddying runs
        in every regime.
\end{itemize}

\section{Data}
\begin{itemize}
  \item 100 Veros ACC runs, $10\times10$ log-spaced grid: $c_k\in[0.0125,\,0.8]$,
        $c_\epsilon\in[0.0875,\,5.6]$; 100 years, 30-day output, interior grid $30\times42\times15$ (x, y, z).
  \item Kept: $T$ and $S$, last 20 years (241 snapshots/run). $S = \SI{35}{psu}$ everywhere in every run.
  \item Nearly deterministic end states: within-window std \SI{0.01}{K} above \SI{1400}{m} vs
        \SI{0.05}{K} between grid neighbours; bottom level still drifts by \SI{0.45}{K} over the window.
  \item One effective parameter: $\;\log c_k - 1.05\,\log c_\epsilon\;$ explains 99\,\% of the variance of
        the domain-mean $T$; the low-$c_k/c_\epsilon$ part of the grid saturates.
  \item Per-level distribution of $T$ (Figure~\ref{fig:tdist}): above \SI{400}{m} a mixture of restoring values
        and interior, with $\sigma_z$ set by the meridional gradient, so $\mu_z \pm 3\sigma_z$ is wider than the
        level and the normalised values stay within $[-0.85,\,0.6]$ ($[-0.7,\,0.4]$ in the top \SI{200}{m});
        below \SI{1400}{m} a warm tail to $x' = +2.7$.
        Run-to-run signal $\sigma_z^{\mathrm{runs}}$: 0.7\,\% of $3\sigma_z$ at the surface, 26\,\% at the bottom.
\end{itemize}

\section{Method}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Fields $\to$ 30 channels $(T_{1..15}, S_{1..15})$, normalised per level, land $\to 0$,
        zero-padded $42\times30 \to 48\times32$:
        \begin{equation}
          x'_{z} = \frac{x_{z} - \mu_{z}}{3\,\max(\sigma_{z}, 0.05)} .
          \label{eq:norm}
        \end{equation}
        $\mu_z, \sigma_z$ are computed once on all 100 runs and held fixed for every hold-out split (a deliberate,
        mild leakage of 30 scaling constants; no held-out state enters training). The sampler clips the predicted clean state at $|x'| \le 1$ (three standard deviations) at every step.
  \item Denoiser: UNet2DModel (64, 64, 128, 128), 6.9\,M parameters. The condition $c$, the standardised
        pair $(\log c_k, \log c_\epsilon)$, enters through an MLP added to the timestep embedding.
  \item DDPM, $\epsilon$-prediction, 1000 steps, squared-cosine schedule, EMA:
        \begin{equation}
          \mathcal L(\theta) = \mathbb E_{t,\,x_0,\,\epsilon}\,
          \big\| \epsilon - \epsilon_\theta\big(\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\,x_0 + \sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\,\epsilon,\; t,\; c\big) \big\|^2 .
        \end{equation}
        20\,000 steps, batch 32, AdamW $10^{-4}$ with cosine decay; \SI{22}{min} on one A100.
  \item Sampling: 1000-step DDPM loop with EMA weights; after every step the land and padding cells are reset to
        the fill at the noise level of that step, $\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\,f + \sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\,z$ (here $f = 0$),
        as those cells looked in training; resetting them to the exact fill (DINO's BorderZeroConstraint) is the
        ablation of Table~\ref{tab:splits}. 32 samples per condition.
\end{itemize}

\section{Evaluation}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Truth: 20-year time-mean state of each held-out run; metrics on water cells.
  \item Baselines: mean training state; nearest training run in standardised log-parameter space;
        average of the axis-neighbour training runs on the grid.
  \item Three hold-out splits, same data file and statistics, different training sets:
        scattered (10 interior points), band of three rows ($c_k$ = 0.126, 0.2, 0.3175; 30 runs; training rows
        a factor 6.3 apart), top row ($c_k$ = 0.8; 10 runs; extrapolation).
  \item Distributional metric. Each state is reduced to its profile of horizontal means over water cells,
        $\bar T_z$. Per level, the Wasserstein-1 distance between the 32 generated values and all 241 true
        snapshots of the window, through the quantile functions,
        \begin{equation}
          W_1^{(z)} = \int_0^1 \big| F_{g,z}^{-1}(q) - F_{t,z}^{-1}(q) \big|\, dq, \qquad
          W_1 = \sum_z \frac{\Delta z_z}{H}\, W_1^{(z)} ,
        \end{equation}
        with $\Delta z_z$ the level thicknesses from the level midpoints ($H = \SI{2080}{m}$).
        All 241 snapshots are used, not a subsample of 32: the quantile form accepts unequal sizes and a
        subsample would only add noise. A point prediction $x$ (the baselines) gives
        $W_1^{(z)} = \frac{1}{241}\sum_i |x_z - \bar T_{z,i}|$. Unlike the RMSE of the ensemble mean, this rewards a correct
        spread and does not reward collapse to the mean; it ignores horizontal structure, which the RMSE keeps.
\end{itemize}

\section{Findings}
\label{sec:findings}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{The land/padding constraint was the main error source.} Same models, sampling only: exact
        zeros re-imposed after every step give 0.152 / 0.135 / \SI{0.173}{K}, zeros re-imposed at the step's noise
        level give 0.061 / 0.076 / \SI{0.141}{K}. In training those cells carry noise like every other; at high
        noise levels the network had never seen a solid block of zeros around the domain. The gain is largest at
        the bottom (0.34 $\to$ \SI{0.07}{K}, scattered) and the earlier mean-state collapse was this artefact: the
        model now sits at 40\,\% of the mean-state error in the scattered split, and $W_1$ at the training grid
        points drops from 0.124 to \SI{0.083}{K}.
  \item \textbf{Model vs baselines.} Band split: beats the nearest run at every level below \SI{26}{m} and in all
        three rows (0.063 / 0.074 / \SI{0.091}{K} vs 0.063 / 0.132 / \SI{0.152}{K} for $c_k$ = 0.126 / 0.2 / 0.3175).
        Top row: beats the nearest run at every level but the surface and the bottom, and copying the row below
        for $c_\epsilon$ 0.14--1.4 (0.05--0.26 vs 0.08--\SI{0.31}{K}); loses at the corner (0.41 vs \SI{0.35}{K}) and in
        the flat regime ($c_\epsilon \ge 3.5$: 0.09--0.10 vs 0.03--\SI{0.08}{K}). Scattered split: loses to the
        one-step neighbours (0.061 vs 0.037 / \SI{0.020}{K}) except between 490 and \SI{650}{m}
        (Figures~\ref{fig:scat}, \ref{fig:band}, \ref{fig:top}).
  \item \textbf{Depth structure.} Error 0.04--\SI{0.08}{K} at every level in the scattered split; the two bottom
        levels are no longer special (\SI{0.07}{K}) except in the top row (\SI{0.29}{K}, driven by the corner runs).
        Worst band: the top \SI{100}{m} (scale $3\sigma_z \approx \SI{12}{K}$ amplifies sampler noise).
  \item \textbf{The sampler's clip is a regulariser, not a range limit.} Same trained models, sampling only
        (exact fill): clip at $|x'|\le 1$ gives 0.152 / \SI{0.173}{K} (scattered / top row), at 1.5 it gives
        0.194 / \SI{0.183}{K}, at 3 it gives 0.302 / \SI{0.277}{K}, although the final samples barely exceed
        $|x'| = 1$ (0.1\,\% of values): clipping the early, inaccurate clean-state estimates keeps the chain on
        track. The price of the clip is a range limit for extrapolation: with the fixed
        statistics 12\,\% of the held-out top row's true bottom values lie above $\mu_z + 3\sigma_z$ and cannot be
        generated; the corner run is the top row's largest error in every variant.
  \item \textbf{Inversions.} Generated 10--12\,\% of interfaces with $\partial_z T < 0$ in every regime;
        truth: 7.7\,\% (scattered) $\to$ 1.3\,\% (band) $\to$ 0.1\,\% (top). Unchanged by the sampler: not learned.
  \item \textbf{$W_1$ on the mean profiles agrees with the RMSE ranking} (right-hand panels of
        Figures~\ref{fig:scat}, \ref{fig:band}, \ref{fig:top}): training grid points score like the held-out ones
        (0.083 vs \SI{0.071}{K}, scattered), and from \SI{490}{m} down the samples beat the point baselines in the
        band and top splits (0.058 vs \SI{0.115}{K} at \SI{650}{m}, band), because a point prediction is charged for
        the spread it cannot represent.
  \item \textbf{Plumbing.} $S$ within \SI{0.02}{psu} of 35 with no constraint; land exact; ensemble spread
        $4\times$ the true within-window spread (Figures~\ref{fig:samples}, \ref{fig:samples_band},
        \ref{fig:samples_top}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Next steps}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Stratification constraint at sampling time (DINO-Fusion's isotonic projection, on $T$ since $S$ is
        constant): targets the inversion miss directly.
  \item Top-row corner: the clip's range limit is now the dominant extrapolation error; a clip that follows the
        conditioning (bounds from the nearest training rows) is the next sampling-only test.
  \item Stronger conditioning: classifier-free guidance; year as a third condition (drift of \SI{0.45}{K}
        at the bottom inside the window). A second training seed to size the seed noise.
  \item Evaluate against snapshots as well as the time mean (nearest-snapshot RMSE, spread--skill).
  \item Then: same pipeline on a global Veros configuration (active $S$, seasonal cycle, multi-century spin-up).
\end{itemize}
