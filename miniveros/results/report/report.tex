\section*{Summary}
We adapted the DINO-Fusion diffusion pipeline to generate the post-spin-up temperature and salinity state of
the Veros ACC channel configuration directly from the two coefficients of its eddy-kinetic-energy closure,
$c_k$ and $c_\epsilon$. The pipeline runs end to end on Jean Zay in under one A100 hour. In this first run,
with DINO-Fusion's per-level normalisation, the conditional model reproduces the mean training state rather
than the parameter dependence: on ten held-out parameter pairs the eight-sample ensemble mean has an RMSE of
\SI{0.154}{K} against the true 20-year mean state, the same as the mean-state baseline (\SI{0.155}{K}),
while the average of the four neighbouring runs on the grid reaches \SI{0.020}{K}. Two harder hold-out
splits, a band of three interior rows and the top row, show that the model's error is nearly independent of
the split while the interpolation baselines degrade with the gap, and reveal a physical miss: the model
generates temperature inversions at the rate of the weakly eddying runs whatever the regime.

\section{Data}
One hundred Veros ACC runs on a complete $10\times10$ log-spaced grid, $c_k\in[0.0125,\,0.8]$ and
$c_\epsilon\in[0.0875,\,5.6]$, 100 years each with 30-day output on a $30\times42\times15$ interior grid
(zonal, meridional, vertical). We keep temperature and salinity for the last 20 years of every run
(241 snapshots per run). Three properties of the ensemble shape the problem:
\begin{itemize}\itemsep1pt
  \item Salinity is exactly \SI{35}{psu} in every water cell of every run; it is carried through the
        pipeline as a second field but contains no information.
  \item The end states are nearly deterministic: the grid-point temporal standard deviation inside the
        window is \SI{0.01}{K} above \SI{1400}{m}, against \SI{0.05}{K} between neighbouring runs on the
        grid and up to \SI{1.5}{K} between extremes. The deep layers are the exception: the bottom level
        still drifts by \SI{0.45}{K} on average over the last 20 years, as much as the spread between
        regimes, so the target there is a slowly moving state rather than an equilibrium.
  \item The two parameters act almost as one: $\log c_k - 1.05\log c_\epsilon$ explains 99\,\% of the
        variance of the domain-mean temperature, and the low-$c_k/c_\epsilon$ part of the grid saturates
        to one state. The response lives between 600 and \SI{800}{m} and near the bottom, not at the
        restored surface.
\end{itemize}
Ten interior grid points were held out at random before any statistic was computed; the remaining 90 runs
(21\,690 snapshots) form the training set.

\section{Method}
Temperature and salinity are stacked into 30 channels, normalised per vertical level as in DINO-Fusion,
$x' = (x-\mu_z)/(3\sigma_z)$ with statistics from the training runs only and a floor on $\sigma_z$ that
keeps the constant salinity at exactly zero, set to zero on the fixed land mask, and zero-padded from
$42\times30$ to $48\times32$ so the UNet can halve the grid four times. With this scaling 99.4\,\% of the
training temperature values lie inside the sampler's clipping range $[-1,1]$; the exceptions are the cold
southern boundary between 650 and \SI{1400}{m} and the two bottom levels, whose extremes are clipped at
sampling. The denoiser is a four-level UNet2DModel (64, 64, 128, 128 channels, 6.9\,M parameters). The
standardised $(\log c_k,\log c_\epsilon)$ pair passes through a small MLP whose output is added to the
timestep embedding. Training is the standard DDPM $\epsilon$-prediction objective with 1000 steps, a
squared-cosine schedule, sample clipping, AdamW at $10^{-4}$ with cosine decay, batch 32, 20\,000 steps and
an EMA of the weights. Sampling runs the 1000-step DDPM loop with the EMA weights and re-imposes zeros on
land and padding after every step.

\section{Findings}
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep1pt
  \item \textbf{The pipeline works end to end.} Extraction, training (22 minutes on one A100), conditional
        generation and evaluation ran as one SLURM dependency chain in under one GPU hour, and every
        component was checked on synthetic and real data before the run.
  \item \textbf{Conditioning is not yet effective.} The ensemble-mean RMSE equals the mean-state baseline:
        the model reproduces the structure shared by all runs and adds noise. The generated map of
        domain-mean temperature over the grid (Figure~\ref{fig:grid}) shows only a faint response in the
        warm high-$c_k$, low-$c_\epsilon$ corner. The mechanism is quantitative: under per-level scaling the
        run-to-run signal is 0.7\,\% of the normalised range at the surface, 9\,\% at \SI{650}{m} and 25\,\%
        at the bottom, and the $\epsilon$-prediction loss weights every cell equally, so training is
        dominated by the shared structure. Consistently, the model beats the mean-state baseline only between
        about 400 and \SI{1400}{m} (Figure~\ref{fig:profile}); it is worse than it in the top \SI{250}{m},
        where the signal is below one percent of the range, and at the two bottom levels, where the training
        distribution is broadened by the residual drift and its extremes are clipped.
  \item \textbf{Spread.} Single samples are \SI{0.188}{K} from the truth and the ensemble spread is three
        times the true within-window spread; the samples in Figure~\ref{fig:samples} look like plausible
        states but differ from the truth at mid-depth and below.
  \item \textbf{Plumbing checks pass.} Salinity returns within \SI{0.01}{psu} of 35 without any constraint,
        land is exact, and the fraction of temperature inversions (9.5\,\%) is close to the truth's (7.7\,\%).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Hold-out splits}
The scattered split gives every held-out run a training neighbour one grid step away on all four sides, the
easiest case for an interpolation baseline. Two harder splits were run with identical settings and 32 samples
per held-out run: a contiguous band of three interior rows ($c_k$ = 0.126, 0.2 and 0.3175, 30 runs, so the
nearest training rows are a factor 6.3 apart in $c_k$) and the top row ($c_k$ = 0.8, 10 runs, pure
extrapolation). Each split has its own training set and normalisation statistics. An additional baseline
interpolates linearly in $\log c_k$ between the nearest training rows below and above in the same $c_\epsilon$
column; for the top row it reduces to copying the row below. The scattered split was re-evaluated with 32
samples for consistency (\SI{0.150}{K} against \SI{0.154}{K} with 8).

Three points follow. First, the model's error is nearly independent of the split (0.13 to \SI{0.19}{K})
while the baselines' error grows with the gap they must bridge (0.02 to \SI{0.17}{K}); the model therefore
catches up as the split gets harder, beats the nearest training run in the two upper rows of the band and over
the trending part of the top row ($c_\epsilon$ from 0.22 to 0.88), but never beats interpolation in
$\log c_k$ over an interior gap. Within the band it is the best method between 650 and \SI{800}{m}
(\SI{0.08}{K} against \SI{0.11}{K} for interpolation); its floor comes from the top \SI{250}{m} and the two
bottom levels, as before. Second, extrapolation is bounded before the model is: under the top split's own
training statistics, 17\,\% of the true bottom-level values of the held-out row lie beyond the sampler's clip
range and cannot be generated, so the generated corner is \SI{0.2}{K} too cold. Third, the inversion rows of
the table show a physical miss that grows with $c_k$: the model generates 10 to 12\,\% of interfaces with
temperature decreasing upward whatever the regime, the rate of the weakly eddying rows that dominate the grid,
whereas in the truth the strongly eddying states are stratified almost everywhere. The model has not learned
that regime dependence, and this is precisely the kind of constraint DINO-Fusion imposes at sampling time.

\section{Next steps}
Impose stratification at sampling time, DINO-Fusion's isotonic projection applied to the temperature profile
since salinity is constant, which targets the inversion miss directly. Strengthen the conditioning within this
normalisation: classifier-free guidance, a longer schedule or a
larger conditioning path, and the year as a third condition so the model knows where in the drift window a
state sits. Evaluate against snapshots as well as the time mean, since the training targets drift by up to
\SI{0.45}{K} at the bottom inside the window. Draw 32 samples per condition, and hold out the whole
$c_k=0.8$ row to test the regime corner. The longer-term target is the same pipeline on a global Veros
configuration, where active salinity, seasonal forcing and multi-century spin-up make the generative
formulation and the density constraints of DINO-Fusion relevant.

\paragraph{Reproducibility.} Branch \texttt{miniveros} of the DINO-Fusion repository, package
\texttt{miniveros/}, commit \texttt{f912e66} for the scattered-split run and \texttt{5991188} for the two
other splits. Extraction takes 2.5 minutes on
8 CPU cores, training 22 minutes on one A100, generation and evaluation 3.5 minutes; the whole dependency
chain ran on the development queue in under one GPU hour.
