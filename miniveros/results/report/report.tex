\section*{Summary}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Goal: generate the post-spin-up $(T, S)$ state of the Veros ACC channel from the EKE-closure
        coefficients $(c_k, c_\epsilon)$, skipping the spin-up.
  \item Pipeline: DINO-Fusion diffusion code adapted to a parameter ensemble; one split runs end to end in
        under one A100 hour.
  \item Interpolation (scattered points, a band of three rows, the $5\times5$ and $7\times7$ centre blocks held
        out): hold-out RMSE 0.061 / 0.076 / 0.061 / \SI{0.064}{K} against 0.037 / 0.116 / 0.077 / \SI{0.114}{K}
        for the nearest training run and 0.155--\SI{0.197}{K} for the mean state. The model's error does not
        depend on the size of the gap; the baselines' does, so the model wins as soon as the gap exceeds one
        grid step.
  \item Extrapolation (the 51 or 75 outer runs held out, training on the centre; the top row): 0.110 / 0.138 /
        \SI{0.141}{K} against 0.131 / 0.155 / \SI{0.170}{K}, medians 0.061 / 0.064. The mean is set by the warm
        corner, where the model is cold-biased (corner run 0.77 and \SI{1.10}{K} when it lies two and three cells
        from the training set); elsewhere 0.04--\SI{0.13}{K}. A Wasserstein metric on the stratification profiles
        gives the same ranking.
  \item Training on the velocity target ($v$-prediction) lets the sampler run without any clip, and improves
        every metric on the two $5\times5$ splits: hold-out RMSE 0.044 and \SI{0.128}{K} (from 0.061 and 0.138),
        single-sample RMSE 0.079 and 0.145 (from 0.153 and 0.213), $W_1$ 0.027 and 0.101 (from 0.070 and 0.118),
        and an ensemble spread of \SI{0.04}{K} against a true \SI{0.03}{K} (from 0.13). The $\epsilon$ models
        diverge without the clip. The warm corner is unchanged: its error is extrapolation, not the clip.
  \item Four training runs only, one step in from each corner: the model still beats the nearest run on average
        (0.154 vs \SI{0.179}{K}) and in $W_1$ (0.106 vs \SI{0.156}{K}) because it interpolates the warm-side trend
        the baseline cannot, but its error is now 0.1--\SI{0.45}{K} over most of the grid.
  \item Physical miss: temperature inversions are generated at the rate of the weakly eddying runs in every
        regime.
\end{itemize}

\section{Data}
\begin{itemize}
  \item 100 Veros ACC runs on a $10\times10$ log-spaced grid, $c_k\in[0.0125,\,0.8]$,
        $c_\epsilon\in[0.0875,\,5.6]$; 100 years, 30-day output; interior grid $30\times42\times15$ (x, y, z).
  \item Kept: $T$ and $S$, last 20 years (241 snapshots per run). $S = \SI{35}{psu}$ everywhere in every run.
  \item Nearly deterministic end states: within-window std \SI{0.01}{K} above \SI{1400}{m} vs
        \SI{0.05}{K} between grid neighbours; the bottom level still drifts by \SI{0.45}{K} over the window.
  \item One effective parameter: $\log c_k - 1.05\,\log c_\epsilon$ explains 99\,\% of the variance of the
        domain-mean $T$; the low-$c_k/c_\epsilon$ part of the grid saturates.
  \item Per-level distribution of $T$ (Figure~\ref{fig:tdist}): above \SI{400}{m} a mixture of restoring
        values and interior, with $\sigma_z$ set by the meridional gradient, so $\mu_z \pm 3\sigma_z$ is wider than
        the level and the normalised values stay within $[-0.85,\,0.6]$; below \SI{1400}{m} a warm tail to
        $x' = +2.7$. Run-to-run signal $\sigma_z^{\mathrm{runs}}$: 0.7\,\% of $3\sigma_z$ at the surface, 26\,\% at
        the bottom. The narrow peaks are the zonally uniform southern rows $y = 0$--$6$, the restoring zone: one
        peak per row, at the same value in every run to within 0.04--\SI{0.18}{K} (Figure~\ref{fig:rows}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Method}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Fields $\to$ 30 channels $(T_{1..15}, S_{1..15})$, normalised per level, land $\to 0$,
        zero-padded $42\times30 \to 48\times32$:
        \begin{equation}
          x'_{z} = \frac{x_{z} - \mu_{z}}{3\,\max(\sigma_{z}, 0.05)} .
          \label{eq:norm}
        \end{equation}
        $\mu_z, \sigma_z$ are computed once on all 100 runs and held fixed for every split (a deliberate, mild
        leakage of 30 scaling constants; no held-out state enters training).
  \item Denoiser: UNet2DModel (64, 64, 128, 128), 6.9\,M parameters. The condition $c$, the standardised pair
        $(\log c_k, \log c_\epsilon)$, enters through an MLP added to the timestep embedding.
  \item DDPM, $\epsilon$-prediction, 1000 steps, squared-cosine schedule, EMA:
        \begin{equation}
          \mathcal L(\theta) = \mathbb E_{t,\,x_0,\,\epsilon}\,
          \big\| \epsilon - \epsilon_\theta\big(\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\,x_0 + \sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\,\epsilon,\; t,\; c\big) \big\|^2 .
        \end{equation}
        20\,000 steps, batch 32, AdamW $10^{-4}$ with cosine decay; \SI{22}{min} on one A100.
  \item Sampling: 1000-step DDPM loop with EMA weights; the predicted clean state is clipped at $|x'| \le 1$;
        after every step the land and padding cells are reset to zero at the step's noise level,
        $\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\,z$, as in training. 32 samples per condition.
  \item Variant: $v$-prediction, the network regresses $v = \sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\,\epsilon - \sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\,x_0$
        instead of $\epsilon$ (same architecture, schedule and steps), and the sampler runs unclipped: the clean-state
        estimate $\hat x_0 = \sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\,x_t - \sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\,\hat v$ stays bounded at high noise, where
        the $\epsilon$ form divides by $\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}$.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Differences from the DINO-Fusion code.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Conditioning: DINO's UNet is unconditional and is steered at sampling time by constraints; here the UNet
        receives $(\log c_k, \log c_\epsilon)$ through the timestep embedding, and the data are an ensemble of 100
        runs split by run instead of one trajectory.
  \item Sampling: DINO's BorderZeroConstraint resets land and padding to exact zeros after every step, which the
        network never sees in training, where those cells carry noise like every other. Here they are reset to
        zeros at the step's noise level, $\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\,z$, exactly as in training. DINO's stratification
        constraints are not ported yet.
  \item Everything else is kept: transform chain, 3-std normalisation, DDPM settings, EMA.
\end{itemize}

\section{Evaluation}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Truth: 20-year time-mean state of each held-out run; metrics on water cells.
  \item Baselines: mean training state; nearest training run in standardised log-parameter space; average of
        the axis-neighbour training runs on the grid.
  \item Eight hold-out splits, same data file and statistics, different training sets. Interpolation:
        scattered (10 interior points), band of three rows ($c_k$ = 0.126, 0.2, 0.3175; 30 runs; training rows a
        factor 6.3 apart), centre $5\times5$ ($c_k$ 0.05--0.32, $c_\epsilon$ 0.35--2.2; 25 runs) and centre $7\times7$
        ($c_k$ 0.0315--0.504, $c_\epsilon$ 0.22--3.5; 49 runs; training on the outer rows and columns only).
        Extrapolation: outer 51 (the complement of the $7\times7$ block, training on its centre), outer 75 (the
        complement of the $5\times5$ block, training on 25 runs), top row ($c_k$ = 0.8; 10 runs). Four runs: training
        on $c_k \in \{0.0198, 0.504\} \times c_\epsilon \in \{0.139, 3.53\}$, one step in from each corner; 96 held out.
  \item Distributional metric: each state is reduced to its profile of horizontal means over water cells,
        $\bar T_z$; per level, the Wasserstein-1 distance between the 32 generated values and the 241 true
        snapshots of the window, through the quantile functions,
        \begin{equation}
          W_1^{(z)} = \int_0^1 \big| F_{g,z}^{-1}(q) - F_{t,z}^{-1}(q) \big|\, dq, \qquad
          W_1 = \sum_z \frac{\Delta z_z}{H}\, W_1^{(z)} ,
        \end{equation}
        with $\Delta z_z$ the level thicknesses ($H = \SI{2080}{m}$). All 241 snapshots are used: the quantile
        form accepts unequal sizes and a subsample of 32 would only add noise. A point prediction $x$ (the
        baselines) gives $W_1^{(z)} = \frac{1}{241}\sum_i |x_z - \bar T_{z,i}|$. $W_1$ rewards a correct spread and
        does not reward collapse to the mean; the RMSE keeps the horizontal structure.
\end{itemize}

\section{Findings}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Interpolation: the model's error is gap-independent, the baselines' is not.} Band: beats the
        nearest run at every level below \SI{26}{m} and in all three rows (0.063 / 0.074 / \SI{0.091}{K} vs
        0.063 / 0.132 / \SI{0.152}{K} for $c_k$ = 0.126 / 0.2 / 0.3175). Centre $5\times5$ and $7\times7$: the same
        error, 0.061 and \SI{0.064}{K}, flat over the block (0.04--\SI{0.12}{K}), while the nearest run goes from
        0.077 to \SI{0.114}{K} and reaches \SI{0.18}{K} at the centre of either block, where the model stays at
        \SI{0.05}{K}. In every split the losses are the runs of the flat regime (low $c_k$, high $c_\epsilon$), where
        a copied neighbour is already within \SI{0.05}{K}; the scattered split, whose neighbours are all one step
        away, is the one where the nearest run wins overall (Figures~\ref{fig:scat}--\ref{fig:block}).
  \item \textbf{Extrapolation: the warm corner sets the mean, the rest behaves like interpolation.} Outer 51 /
        outer 75 / top row: mean 0.110 / 0.138 / \SI{0.141}{K}, median 0.061 / 0.064 / \SI{0.11}{K}. The corner run
        ($c_k$ = 0.8, $c_\epsilon$ = 0.0875) errs by 0.77, 1.10 and \SI{0.41}{K} with a cold bias ($-0.43$, $-0.68$,
        \SI{-0.19}{K}): the model does not carry the warming beyond the last training row, and the error grows
        with the distance to it (two, three and one cells). Away from the corner the outer 75 sit at
        \SI{0.089}{K} against \SI{0.095}{K} for the nearest run. The model beats the nearest run from about
        \SI{150}{m} to the bottom and at 24 / 51, 32 / 75 and 7 / 10 runs; the nearest run is worse still at the
        corner (0.92 and \SI{1.24}{K}) (Figures~\ref{fig:ring}--\ref{fig:top}).
  \item \textbf{Four training runs.} Mean 0.154 vs \SI{0.179}{K} for the nearest run and \SI{0.254}{K} for the
        mean state, $W_1$ 0.106 vs \SI{0.156}{K}, but median 0.120 vs \SI{0.070}{K} and 31 wins of 96. The model
        produces a smooth field over the grid, 0.05--\SI{0.16}{K} in the flat regime and 0.2--\SI{0.45}{K} on the warm
        side with a warm bias of up to \SI{+0.27}{K} between the two $c_k$ rows it was trained on, and \SI{0.70}{K} at
        the corner (cold). The nearest run is far better where the state hardly changes (0.01--\SI{0.07}{K}) and far
        worse where it does (0.5--\SI{0.85}{K} in the warm half of the interior), so the two average out differently:
        inside the training rectangle 0.159 vs \SI{0.201}{K}, outside 0.146 vs \SI{0.143}{K}. Below \SI{250}{m} the
        model wins at every level (Figure~\ref{fig:four}).
  \item \textbf{Depth structure.} 0.03--\SI{0.08}{K} at every level in the interpolation splits; the bottom
        levels are no longer special (0.05--\SI{0.07}{K}) except where the warm corner is held out (top row
        \SI{0.29}{K}, outer 51 \SI{0.17}{K}, outer 75 \SI{0.19}{K}).
  \item \textbf{Why the surface is worst in kelvin.} The per-level scale $3\sigma_z$ is \SI{12}{K} at the surface
        and \SI{1.6}{K} at the bottom, and the sampler's precision is roughly uniform in normalised units: the
        surface RMSE is 0.6\,\% of its scale (\SI{0.073}{K}, scattered), against 1\,\% at \SI{650}{m} and 4.5\,\% at
        the bottom. The run-to-run surface signal is 0.7\,\% of the scale (\SI{0.085}{K}), the same size, so the
        model cannot tell the runs apart there and matches the mean state (0.076 vs \SI{0.073}{K}); the nearest
        run wins (\SI{0.026}{K}) only because neighbouring runs are nearly identical at the surface. What remains
        is a per-sample offset of the surface mean: $W_1$ on the horizontal-mean profile is \SI{0.12}{K} at
        \SI{14}{m}, above the \SI{0.07}{K} cell RMSE of the ensemble mean, so each sample carries a nearly uniform
        shift of about \SI{0.1}{K} (0.01 normalised) that the average of 32 mostly removes.
  \item \textbf{$v$-prediction removes the clip and halves the single-sample error.} Retrained on the two
        $5\times5$ splits with the velocity target and sampled unclipped: centre $5\times5$ 0.044 vs \SI{0.061}{K},
        outer 75 0.128 vs \SI{0.138}{K} for the $\epsilon$ models with the clip; single samples 0.079 / 0.145 vs
        0.153 / \SI{0.213}{K}; $W_1$ 0.027 / 0.101 vs 0.070 / \SI{0.118}{K}; the ensemble spread falls from 0.13 to
        \SI{0.04}{K}, close to the true within-window \SI{0.03}{K}, so the $\epsilon$ models were overdispersed by the
        sampler, not by the data. Wins against the nearest run rise from 11 to 15 of 25 and from 32 to 44 of 75,
        with the flat regime now at 0.03--\SI{0.05}{K}. Grid $W_1$ at the training points drops from 0.087 to
        \SI{0.028}{K}. The warm corner does not move (1.03 vs \SI{1.10}{K} at the corner run, cold bias): its error is
        the extrapolation itself, not the clip. Control: the $\epsilon$ models sampled without clip diverge
        (hold-out RMSE above \SI{500}{K}), so under $\epsilon$-prediction the clip is what keeps the chain finite.
        One caveat: every $v$-prediction sample carries a small spot of wrong values at the tip of the ridge, visible
        in $S$ (\SI{0.03}{psu}) and at the surface in $T$ (Figure~\ref{fig:samples_block5v}); the salinity error
        rises from 0.013 to \SI{0.046}{psu} (Figures~\ref{fig:block5v}, \ref{fig:ring5v}).
  \item \textbf{The clip trades regularisation for range ($\epsilon$-prediction).} Same models, sampling only, scattered / top row:
        clip at $|x'| \le 1$ gives 0.061 / \SI{0.141}{K}, at 1.5 it gives 0.061 / \SI{0.108}{K}, at 3 it gives
        0.091 / \SI{0.123}{K}; $W_1$ 0.066 / 0.132, 0.086 / 0.117, 0.147 / \SI{0.154}{K}. Widening to 1.5 costs
        nothing in interpolation RMSE, widens the sample spread ($W_1$), and frees the top-row corner, where 12\,\%
        of the true bottom values lie above $\mu_z + 3\sigma_z$ and cannot be generated under the clip at 1; at 3
        the regularisation is lost (0.091 and \SI{0.147}{K} in the scattered split) and without any clip the chain
        diverges. The corner run is the largest error of the top-row and outer splits whatever the clip.
  \item \textbf{Inversions.} 10--12\,\% of interfaces with $\partial_z T < 0$ in every regime; truth 7.7\,\%
        (scattered), 6.6 and 6.1\,\% (outer 51 and 75), 4.2 and 3.6\,\% (centre $7\times7$ and $5\times5$), 1.3\,\%
        (band), 0.1\,\% (top). Not learned.
  \item \textbf{$W_1$ agrees with the RMSE ranking.} Training grid points score like held-out ones (0.083 vs
        \SI{0.071}{K}, scattered); from \SI{490}{m} down the samples beat the point baselines in the band and top
        splits (0.058 vs \SI{0.115}{K} at \SI{650}{m}, band), which pay for the spread they cannot represent.
  \item \textbf{Plumbing.} $S$ within \SI{0.02}{psu} of 35 with no constraint; land exact; ensemble spread
        $4\times$ the true within-window spread (Figures~\ref{fig:samples}--\ref{fig:samples_top}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Next steps}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Retrain the remaining splits with $v$-prediction and no clip, and make it the default.
  \item Stratification constraint at sampling time (DINO's isotonic projection on $T$): targets the inversions.
  \item Warm corner: unchanged by $v$-prediction, so it is a limit of the extrapolation, not of the sampler;
        stronger conditioning or an explicit trend prior is the lever.
  \item Stronger conditioning: classifier-free guidance; the year as a third condition (bottom drift of
        \SI{0.45}{K} inside the window). A second training seed to size the seed noise.
  \item Evaluate against snapshots as well as the time mean (nearest-snapshot RMSE, spread--skill).
  \item Then the same pipeline on a global Veros configuration (active $S$, seasonal cycle, multi-century spin-up).
\end{itemize}
